# How to Stand Out

[← Back to README](../README.md) · [← Common Mistakes](06-COMMON-MISTAKES.md)

If your project just works, you get points. If it tells a story, you win. Here is how you engineer a demo that judges actually care about.

| Rule | The Execution | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Tell a Story** | Don't show features. Walk through a scenario: *"Imagine you are a sales rep on your 5th call with a prospect..."* | Judges need to feel the pain before they care about the solution. |
| **2. Show the Learning Curve** | Show Interaction 1 (generic), Interaction 5 (personalized), and Interaction 20 (feels like it knows you). | Proves your backend state/memory actually works. That progression is what makes memory-powered agents exciting. |
| **3. The LinkedIn Test** | Build something you would proudly post on LinkedIn tomorrow. If you wouldn't, rethink it. | Forces you to build real utility. The best hackathon projects get written up as case studies. |
| **4. Keep Scope Tight** | One workflow. One persona. One clear value prop. | A polished agent that does one thing brilliantly beats a sprawling prototype that does five things poorly. |
| **5. Use Realistic Data** | Use Kaggle/HF data, or LLM-generate synthetic logs. Give customers real names, deals real numbers, and incidents real error logs. | The #1 thing that makes a project look real is data. `User: test1` kills credibility. |
